# The Profit Tree

*A working outline for a higher-education book on strategic profitability*

## Concept and framing

This book is aimed at higher-education students and practitioners, written in a popular-science register rather than as a conventional Norwegian "fagbok": rigorous enough to teach the underlying technical tools, but written to be read, not just referenced. Throughout, profitability is framed through a single extended metaphor — **the Profit Tree**.

The book's central idea: profitability is not only about a positive bottom line. It is about assessing a firm's **potential** — given its industry and its own strategic assets — and then evaluating actual performance *against that potential* as the benchmark. Every part of the tree (trunk, branches, leaves/berries, roots) is analyzed both for what it currently delivers and for what it *could* deliver.

| Tree part | Part | Analytical lens |
|---|---|---|
| Trunk & setting | Part 1 | Strategy analysis — the forest and the tree |
| Branches | Part 2 | Operational analysis (costing) |
| Leaves & Berries | Part 3 | Revenue analysis (pricing) |
| Roots | Part 4 | Sustainability, ethics & the human/organizational system |

Each part blends technical method (frameworks, models, calculations) with strategic and managerial judgment — the "how to compute it" and the "how to decide with it."

A note on numbering: chapters are numbered continuously 1–15 across the four parts. The introduction and closing are unnumbered framing chapters, not part of a "module."
