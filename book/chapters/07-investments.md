# Chapter 7: Investments

**Part:** Part 2 — Pruning the Branches
**Tree element:** The branches — growth decisions that commit both OPEX and CAPEX at once.

## Premise

This chapter combines the OPEX analysis (Chapter 5) and CAPEX analysis (Chapter 6) into investment decisions. It is deliberately not a long, freestanding introduction to investment appraisal — the point is to show that many real profit decisions require holding both OPEX and CAPEX thinking in mind simultaneously.

## Content to cover

- Frame investment decisions explicitly as the natural meeting point of Chapters 5 and 6: an investment decision almost always trades off an upfront/ongoing capital commitment against an operating cost or capacity profile.
- Walk through one or two representative investment decisions (e.g., automation that raises CAPEX and capacity while lowering OPEX; or a make/lease-type choice) showing both analyses applied together.
- Keep the treatment of formal investment-appraisal mechanics (NPV, payback, etc.) light — reference them as tools the reader may already know, rather than teaching them from scratch.
- Close Part 2 by tying back to Chapter 3's problem-structuring toolkit and Chapter 4's cost-causality principle: this chapter is where all of Part 2's pieces are used together on one decision.

## Key frameworks & references

- Combined OPEX/CAPEX decision framing (this chapter does not introduce new named frameworks; it integrates Chapters 5–6)

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Chapters 5 and 6 directly — this chapter has no independent content beyond their combination.
- **Closes:** Part 2, before the transition to Part 3 (revenue/pricing).

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- Per the brief, do **not** write a long, self-contained investment-appraisal chapter — assume the reader may already know NPV/payback and focus on the OPEX+CAPEX integration, not on teaching appraisal mechanics from first principles.
- One or two well-chosen worked examples should carry the whole chapter; avoid a survey of many investment types.
- Use this chapter's ending as the explicit close of Part 2 — a short recap connecting Chapters 4–7 (measurement principle → OPEX method → CAPEX method → combined decision) helps the reader feel the arc before moving to Part 3.
- Keep it shorter than Chapters 4–6; it is an integrative capstone, not a new topic.
