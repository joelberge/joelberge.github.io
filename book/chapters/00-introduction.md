# Introduction: Planting the Tree

**Position:** Unnumbered framing chapter, before Part 1.
**Tree element:** The whole tree, seen from a distance.

## Premise

This book is not primarily about how to get a positive bottom line. It is about assessing a firm's **potential** — what profitability it *could* achieve given its industry and its own strategic assets — and then using that potential as the benchmark against which actual performance is judged. A firm can be "profitable" and still be underperforming its potential; a firm can be loss-making and still be outperforming a brutal environment. The whole book is built to make that comparison possible, part by part.

## What this chapter must do

- State the potential-vs-performance idea plainly, with one simple illustrative example (e.g., two firms with identical accounting profit but very different potential given their industries/resources).
- Introduce the Profit Tree metaphor and preview its four parts (trunk & forest, branches, leaves & berries, roots) at a conceptual level only — no techniques yet.
- Explain, as a subheading ("How to read this book"), the recurring structure readers will meet: conceptual framing → framework/technique → strategic implication, plus how technical boxes, vignettes, and cases are used.
- Set expectations on prior knowledge: readable by a motivated student without an accounting or strategy background, but useful to one who has it.

## Connections

- **Sets up:** the industry-potential idea (Ch. 1), the firm-potential idea (Ch. 2), and the closing chapter's return to potential-vs-performance as an integrating device.

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- Keep this chapter short — it is a front door, not a summary of the book. Resist the urge to preview every framework; name them, don't teach them yet.
- Popular-science register: open with a concrete story or contrast, not a definition.
- The "how to read this book" subsection should be practical and brief (half a page), not a formal preface.
- Do not introduce Porter, RBV, ABC, or other named frameworks by name yet beyond a passing mention in the tree-parts preview — save the technical vocabulary for the chapters that own it.
- End on the potential-vs-performance question so it lands as the thread the whole book pulls on.
