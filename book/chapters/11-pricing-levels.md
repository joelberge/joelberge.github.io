# Chapter 11: Pricing Levels

**Part:** Part 3 — The Leaves and the Berries
**Tree element:** The berries — setting today's price.

## Premise

This chapter closes Part 3 by turning to a concrete, actionable question: how do we assess today's pricing levels and structure using economic theory?

## Content to cover

- **Inverse price elasticity (rule):** using demand elasticity to inform relative pricing across products/segments — the more inelastic the demand, the higher the sustainable relative price.
- **Relative break-even analysis:** assessing how large a volume change a price change can tolerate before profitability is harmed, as a practical decision-support tool.
- **Price discrimination and dynamic pricing:** structuring prices across customers/segments/time to capture more of the value identified back in Chapter 9, tying the economic techniques back to customer value and profitability measurement (Chapters 9–10).
- Frame the chapter as the technical/quantitative capstone of Part 3, following the conceptual (Ch. 8), value-driver (Ch. 9), and measurement (Ch. 10) chapters.

## Key frameworks & references

- Inverse elasticity rule
- Relative break-even analysis
- Price discrimination (1st/2nd/3rd degree) and dynamic pricing

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Chapter 8 (pricing methods), Chapter 9 (customer value), Chapter 10 (customer profitability).
- **Closes:** Part 3, before the transition to Part 4 (roots/ethics), where Chapter 13 revisits pricing through a behavioral lens.

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- This is the most formula-driven chapter in Part 3 — balance the economic technique with a return, at the end of each technique, to "so what does this mean strategically," so it doesn't read as a pure micro-economics chapter transplanted into the book.
- Worked numeric examples are essential here (inverse elasticity applied to two products with different elasticities; a break-even sensitivity table; a simple price-discrimination example) — this chapter is where readers should come away able to actually run a calculation.
- Be careful and explicit about the ethical/perception edge of price discrimination and dynamic pricing (e.g., fairness perceptions, potential backlash) even briefly — it sets up Chapter 13's "pricing for humans" theme and Part 4's ethics discussion so the transition doesn't feel abrupt.
- Close Part 3 with a short recap tying Chapters 8–11 together (strategy → value → measurement → level-setting) before moving into Part 4.
