# Chapter 5: Performance of Operations (OPEX)

**Part:** Part 2 — Pruning the Branches
**Tree element:** The branches — how well they currently bear weight.

## Premise

This chapter operationalizes Chapter 4's cost-causality principle for operating expenses, and separately addresses a related but distinct managerial question: how much operating capacity a firm has, and should have.

## Content to cover

**Part A — Costing techniques:**
- Activity-Based Costing (ABC): principle and mechanics.
- Time-Driven Activity-Based Costing (TDABC): what it improves on ABC, and its own weaknesses.
- Present both fairly, including their practical weaknesses (implementation cost, precision-vs-simplicity trade-offs, risk of false precision) — this is not a sales pitch for ABC/TDABC.

**Part B — Capacity:**
- Determining available capacity is a crucial decision that is more managerial than technical.
- Present a framework for determining capacity (practical capacity vs. theoretical vs. normal capacity, and the judgment calls behind choosing among them).
- Make explicit the connection back to Part A: capacity assumptions directly affect the "rate" used in ABC/TDABC allocations, so the two halves of the chapter are linked, not just adjacent.

## Key frameworks & references

- Activity-Based Costing (ABC)
- Time-Driven Activity-Based Costing (TDABC)
- Capacity concepts: theoretical, practical, normal capacity

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Chapter 4's cost-causality principle — this chapter is where that principle becomes a concrete method.
- **Parallels:** Chapter 6, which does the same operationalization for CAPEX instead of OPEX — the two chapters should feel structurally parallel.
- **Sets up:** Chapter 7 (Investments), which needs both OPEX and CAPEX performance measures together.

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- Structure as two clearly labeled halves (costing technique, then capacity) rather than interleaving — the brief treats them as related-but-separate, and mixing them will confuse the throughline.
- Be genuinely even-handed about ABC/TDABC's weaknesses (cost of implementation, the illusion of precision, maintenance burden) — the book's credibility with practitioner-minded readers depends on not overselling any one technique.
- The capacity section should foreground the *managerial judgment* angle promised in the brief — this is not a formula-only section; show how the same operational facts support different capacity choices depending on strategic intent (e.g., growth vs. efficiency).
- A short worked numeric example (one activity, ABC vs. TDABC side by side) will help ground Part A before moving to the more conceptual capacity discussion in Part B.
