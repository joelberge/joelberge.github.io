# Chapter 10: Customer Profitability Analyses

**Part:** Part 3 — The Leaves and the Berries
**Tree element:** The berries — weighing the harvest, past and future.

## Premise

Having established what drives customer value (Chapter 9), this chapter measures customer profitability itself, across two dimensions: time direction (backward- vs. forward-looking) and level of aggregation (individual vs. segment). The four resulting combinations structure the chapter.

## Content to cover

Present and work through all four quadrants explicitly:

- **Backward-looking, individual level:** measuring realized profitability of a specific customer using historical data.
- **Backward-looking, segment level:** measuring realized profitability of a customer segment using historical data.
- **Forward-looking, individual level:** projecting expected future profitability of a specific customer (e.g., customer lifetime value logic).
- **Forward-looking, segment level:** projecting expected future profitability of a customer segment.
- Discuss what changes in method and data requirements as you move along each dimension (backward vs. forward; individual vs. segment) — this 2×2 structure is the chapter's organizing device, not just a list.
- Tie back to Chapter 4's cost-allocation principle (cost causality) — customer profitability measurement is a direct application of that principle to the customer dimension rather than the product dimension.

## Key frameworks & references

- The backward/forward × individual/segment 2×2 framework for customer profitability
- Customer lifetime value (as the natural forward-looking, individual-level concept)

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Chapter 9's value-driver analysis and Chapter 4's cost-causality principle.
- **Parallels:** conceptually mirrors Chapter 4's earlier "how do we even measure this" caution — the same measurement humility applies to customer profitability.
- **Sets up:** Chapter 11, which uses profitability insights to inform actual price-level decisions.

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- Use the 2×2 (backward/forward × individual/segment) as an explicit visual organizing device early in the chapter (flag as a candidate figure/table) so readers can place each sub-section within it as they read.
- Give each of the four quadrants a short worked or illustrative example — uneven treatment (e.g., three paragraphs on backward-individual and one sentence on forward-segment) will make the 2×2 feel like a gimmick rather than a real framework.
- Flag data-availability realism throughout: backward-looking analyses need decent cost-allocation data (link to Ch. 4–5), forward-looking analyses need assumptions about retention/behavior — be honest about the added uncertainty in the forward-looking quadrants.
- Keep the customer lifetime value treatment conceptual/applied rather than a deep dive into discounting mechanics — this is a customer-strategy book, not a finance text.
