# Chapter 9: Customer Analysis

**Part:** Part 3 — The Leaves and the Berries
**Tree element:** The berries — understanding who eats them and why.

## Premise

This chapter is about matching costs and benefits again — but the emphasis shifts firmly to understanding the customer as a strategic target: what drives value for them, and then aligning that with what it costs the company to deliver on the attributes customers actually care about.

## Content to cover

- Customer analysis as a strategic exercise: identifying what specific attributes/benefits drive value for different customers or segments (not just "the customer" in the abstract).
- Connecting customer value drivers to the company's cost side: for each attribute the customer values, what does it cost to deliver?
- **Target costing / target pricing:** pricing based on customer value, then engineering the product/service to hit a threshold cost that preserves the target margin — i.e., working backward from customer-perceived value to an allowable cost, rather than forward from cost to price.
- Distinguish this chapter's customer-value focus from Chapter 10's profitability-measurement focus: this chapter is about understanding *value drivers*, Chapter 10 is about measuring *realized profitability* per customer/segment.

## Key frameworks & references

- Customer value driver analysis
- Target costing / target pricing

## Connections

- **Builds on:** Chapter 8's customer-based pricing thread.
- **Parallels:** Chapter 4's cost-causality principle, now applied from the customer-value side rather than the internal-cost side.
- **Sets up:** Chapter 10 (Customer Profitability Analyses), which measures the outcome once products/services are designed and priced.

## Writing guidelines (for the author)

- Keep the customer-value-driver discussion concrete: use a segmentation example where different customer groups clearly value different attributes, so "value" doesn't stay abstract.
- Target costing/target pricing is the technical payoff of the chapter — give it a proper worked example (a threshold cost derived from a target price and target margin, then a product/service redesigned to hit it).
- Be explicit about the direction of reasoning (value → allowable cost) since it inverts the more familiar cost → price logic from Chapter 8's cost-based method; a short contrast box would help readers see the inversion clearly.
- Keep this chapter distinct from Chapter 10 — this one is forward-looking and design-oriented (what should we build and for whom), Chapter 10 is backward/forward measurement-oriented (how profitable are our actual customers).
